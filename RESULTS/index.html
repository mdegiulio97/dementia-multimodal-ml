    Results (v0.1)

    Each number below comes from a result file in results/ that records its own provenance (dataset version or checksum, git commit, configuration, seed). Comparisons with published work cite only papers whose records were checked (see DATA_SOURCES.md, section 6).

    Marks: ✓ consistent with the reference; ⚠ consistent with caveats; ✗ inconsistent.

    Resting-state EEG: AD, FTD and controls (OpenNeuro ds004504)

    88 participants, 34,788 four-second epochs. All metrics are per participant and come from 5 × 5-fold CV split by participant, with 95% bootstrap intervals. The intervals resample participants over pooled out-of-fold predictions, so they do not include the variance from refitting the models.

    Task n Model AUC Balanced accuracy
    AD vs CN 65 logistic 0.91 (0.82-0.97) 0.81 (0.70-0.91)
    AD vs CN 65 LightGBM 0.90 (0.81-0.97) 0.81 (0.70-0.91)
    FTD vs CN 52 logistic 0.74 (0.59-0.87) 0.65 (0.53-0.76)
    FTD vs CN 52 LightGBM 0.83 (0.70-0.94) 0.73 (0.61-0.84)
    AD vs FTD 59 logistic 0.61 (0.44-0.76) 0.57 (0.48-0.66)
    AD vs FTD 59 LightGBM 0.53 (0.36-0.69) 0.59 (0.51-0.69)
    3-class 88 LightGBM 0.74 (0.66-0.82) (one-vs-rest) 0.63 (0.54-0.71)

    Comparison with the literature. The AHEPA benchmark (Miltiadous et al. 2026, doi:10.1007/s11571-026-10464-w) averages the 12 subject-level studies on this dataset.

    • AD vs CN ✓. The benchmark reports 82.1% accuracy, and our balanced accuracy is 0.81. Mlinarič et al. 2026 (doi:10.1038/s41598-026-35316-9, leave-one-subject-out) report ROC-AUC 0.82; we get 0.90-0.91.
    • FTD vs CN ✓. The benchmark reports 75.2% accuracy; our balanced accuracy is 0.73 with LightGBM.
    • AD vs FTD ⚠. The benchmark reports 71.4% accuracy, but that number disagrees with its own table. Mlinarič report AUC 0.65. We are close to chance.

    Studies that let epochs of one person cross folds report 9-17 points more. The benchmark names this leakage as the reason; our splits exclude it by construction and a test checks it.

    Limitations. - One small single-centre dataset, with no external test set. - We used the preprocessing shipped with the dataset. - In these recordings most power lies below 2 Hz, so relative delta power is about 0.88 in every group. The spectrum of two controls was checked and the dominance is a property of the data, not a computation error. Phase 2 adds a sensitivity analysis with delta at 1-4 Hz.

    Sensitivity analyses and deep learning (phase 2)

    Delta from 1 Hz instead of 0.5 Hz. The results do not change.

    Task Model AUC, 0.5-45 Hz AUC, 1-45 Hz
    AD vs CN logistic 0.91 0.90 (0.81-0.97)
    AD vs CN LightGBM 0.90 0.90 (0.80-0.97)
    FTD vs CN logistic 0.74 0.76 (0.60-0.88)
    FTD vs CN LightGBM 0.83 0.83 (0.70-0.94)

    Even from 1 Hz, delta keeps about 80% of the relative power in every group (medians 0.80-0.82). The slow-activity dominance is a property of these recordings, not of the band edge.

    EEGNet on raw 4-s windows (125 Hz, z-scored per channel). It uses the same outer folds by participant, early stopping on an inner split that is also by participant, and one prediction per participant.

    Task n EEGNet AUC EEGNet balanced accuracy Best spectral model AUC
    AD vs CN 65 (13,282 windows) 0.81 (0.69-0.90) 0.63 (0.53-0.73) 0.91
    FTD vs CN 52 (10,152 windows) 0.64 (0.48-0.78) 0.56 (0.46-0.66) 0.83

    With 52-65 participants, the convolutional network does worse than spectral features. Its training loss falls from 0.63 to 0.17, while performance on held-out participants stays below the spectral models. The network fits the participants it has seen. Thousands of windows do not make up for few people, because the windows of one person are not independent examples. This is the expected behaviour of deep learning on small clinical cohorts, and it is why the spectral models remain the primary EEG result.

    Mendelian randomisation: circulating proteins and metabolites on Alzheimer's disease

    Inputs. - Exposures: UKB-PPP cis-pQTL (Sun et al. 2023, doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06592-6) and NMR metabolite QTL (Karjalainen et al. 2024, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07148-y). - Outcome: Bellenguez et al. 2022 (doi:10.1038/s41588-022-01024-z, GCST90027158).

    Instruments. We excluded the APOE region and pruned to one variant per 500 kb.

    Family Tested FDR < 0.05 Selected for ADNI
    Proteins (cis-pQTL) 1,584 44 44
    Metabolites (NMR) 233 7 6

    Strongest protein signals. CR1, TREM2, PILRA/PILRB, CD2AP, GRN, EPHA1, ACE, CD55, CD33 and others. - Most of these sit in known AD GWAS loci. That is expected, because a cis-pQTL at a GWAS locus gives a strong Wald ratio. - It is also the main caveat: without colocalisation, the protein and the disease may share a locus without sharing a causal variant. - The full table is results/open/mr_candidates.csv, and the list passed to ADNI is results/open/mr_candidates_selected.txt.

    Metabolites. The seven FDR-significant traits are lipoprotein composition measures and glutamine, each with 18-47 instruments, so the sensitivity estimators apply. Six (Gln, L-VLDL-TG%, M-LDL-FC%, M-LDL-PL%, XS-VLDL-FC, XXL-VLDL-CE%) are passed to ADNI. L-VLDL-C% is held back because its Egger intercept is borderline (p = 0.049).

    Known-answer check ✓. For CD33 we estimate +0.032 log-odds of AD per SD (95% CI 0.017-0.047). This is the same direction as the colocalised estimate in Zheng et al. 2020 (doi:10.1038/s41588-020-0682-6, Table S7: +0.100). The magnitudes differ because the studies use different pQTL sources, outcome GWAS and scaling.

    Sensitivity: an AD GWAS without UK Biobank ✓. UK Biobank is in both the pQTL study and Bellenguez 2022, so the screen was repeated with Kunkle et al. 2019 (doi:10.1038/s41588-019-0358-2, GCST007511, no UK Biobank). - Kunkle has no allele frequencies, so every palindromic instrument is dropped (1,100). - Of the 50 selected molecules, 45 could be tested: - 42 keep the same sign, and 26 of them reach p < 0.05; - FES, ARHGAP45 and LRP11 change sign, all with Kunkle p > 0.3; - TREM2, CD2AP, F13B, FCRLB and SULT1A1 have no usable instrument. - Nine proteins pass FDR with Kunkle: CR1, EPHA1, CELA2A, PILRA, PILRB, C1S, C1R, CD33 and IL34. All but CELA2A were already selected. - Across all 1,465 traits tested with both outcomes, the estimates correlate at r = 0.42, mostly noise around zero; the selected molecules sit along the diagonal (figures/open/mr_bellenguez_vs_kunkle.png).

    The sample overlap therefore does not create the signal: the selected molecules keep their direction in an independent GWAS.

    Colocalisation of the strongest candidates ✓/⚠. The question is whether the protein and AD share the same causal variant. If they do not, the MR signal could come from linkage disequilibrium with a different variant.

    Method. coloc ABF (Giambartolomei et al. 2014, doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1004383): priors p1 = p2 = 1e-4 and p12 = 1e-5, a ±500 kb window around the cis lead variant, UKB-PPP European discovery regional summary statistics against Bellenguez 2022 (GRCh38). It was run on the nine candidates that held up with Kunkle 2019.

    • Seven proteins colocalise strongly (PP.H4 > 0.93): CR1, PILRA/PILRB, IL34, C1S/C1R and CD33.
    • CD33 matches the known answer ✓. Zheng et al. 2020 also report CD33 as colocalised.
    • ACE and EPHA1 do not colocalise under ABF, which assumes one causal variant per trait. The two loci were therefore re-analysed with SuSiE (below).

    SuSiE-based colocalisation for ACE and EPHA1.

    Method. - Fine-mapping with SuSiE-RSS (Zou et al. 2022, doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1010299; L = 10) for each trait. - The LD comes from UK Biobank (Alkes Price group, s3://broad-alkesgroup-ukbb-ld, GRCh37), the same population as UKB-PPP. - Colocalisation is computed for every pair of credible sets (Wallace 2021, doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1009440). - Implemented in Python in dementia_ml.mr.susie and tested on simulated loci with a known answer.

    Protein Variants Credible sets (protein / AD) Best PP.H4 MR, strongest pQTL MR, colocalising signal
    ACE 3,072 5 / 1 0.98 −0.12 (SE 0.018) −0.19 (−0.24 to −0.14), p = 2×10⁻¹⁵
    EPHA1 2,417 10 / 1 0.008 −0.29 (SE 0.043) none
    • ACE colocalises ✓. The ACE locus carries five independent signals for plasma ACE. One of them, not the strongest, is shared with AD (PP.H4 = 0.98). The ABF result was an artefact of its single-variant assumption.
    • Corrected ACE estimate. The first MR estimate used the strongest pQTL, which is not the AD signal. With the colocalising variant as instrument (17:61548948 C/A, GRCh37) the estimate is −0.19 log-odds of AD per SD of plasma ACE, stronger than before and in the same direction. It is also the same direction as in ADNI CSF, where higher ACE predicts lower conversion (HR 0.76).
    • EPHA1 does not colocalise ✗, even with multiple signals allowed. Its MR signal most likely reflects LD with a different causal variant, so EPHA1 is no longer counted among the credible candidates.
    • Caveat ⚠. Ten credible sets for EPHA1 at n ≈ 34,000 point to sensitivity to small LD differences. The reference is UK Biobank, as are the pQTLs, which limits this.

    Epitope-effect check ⚠. A variant that changes the protein sequence can change how the Olink antibody binds the protein without changing its amount. In that case the cis-pQTL partly measures binding, not abundance.

    Method. For each colocalised protein, the instrument and its 1000 Genomes EUR proxies (r² ≥ 0.8) were annotated with Ensembl VEP. Protein-altering means missense, stop, start-lost, frameshift, in-frame indel or a splice donor/acceptor variant in the gene encoding the protein.

    For five of the eight proteins the pQTL may partly reflect antibody binding. The colocalisation still holds: the variant is shared with AD. What weakens is the step from "this variant" to "the amount of this protein". A protein-altering variant can also be truly functional, and this check cannot tell the two apart. ACE and C1R are clean.

    ACE is the only candidate supported by four independent checks: 1. MR with a colocalising instrument; 2. colocalisation of a shared variant; 3. no protein-altering variant in LD with the instrument; 4. the same direction in CSF in an independent cohort (ADNI).

    Correcting a harmonisation bug mattered. An earlier version flipped the sign of palindromic instruments (A/T, C/G) whose alleles were listed in the opposite order. This corrupted the multi-instrument metabolite estimates (0 significant before the fix, 7 after), while the single-instrument protein results stayed the same. Tests now cover both orderings and the strand flip.

    Excluding the APOE region mattered. In the first run, proteins next to APOE (BCAM, NECTIN2, PVR, CEACAM19) passed FDR. They disappear once APOE-region instruments are dropped, as they should if APOE drives them.

    Limitations. - Almost every protein has one instrument, so Egger and the weighted median cannot be estimated. - There is no colocalisation. - UK Biobank is in both the exposure and the outcome GWAS (sample overlap). - Pruning is by distance, not by LD.

    MCI-to-dementia conversion

    Method check on the synthetic cohort

    The synthetic cohort has 1,500 participants with an ADNIMERGE-like structure. Of these, 787 had MCI at baseline with follow-up, and 225 converted. They are generated from a known Weibull hazard. The metrics are out-of-fold, from 5-fold CV split by participant.

    Cox, age + MMSE + APOE4 0.64 (0.60-0.68) 0.67 (0.62-0.71) 0.68 0.66
    Cox (ridge), all baseline 0.72 (0.68-0.76) 0.74 (0.69-0.78) 0.76 0.76
    Random survival forest 0.70 (0.66-0.73) 0.71 (0.66-0.75) 0.73 0.74
    Gradient-boosted survival 0.69 (0.65-0.73) 0.70 (0.65-0.74) 0.71 0.73

    What should happen does happen: - The full model beats the baseline, because the simulated hazard uses ADAS13 and hippocampal volume. - The correctly specified Cox model is not beaten by the flexible models. - Calibration at 36 months follows the diagonal. - The Cox coefficients recover the simulated log hazard ratios within ±0.15 (test).

    The 48-month horizon is skipped because it equals the end of follow-up.

    Reference for the real-data run. Wang et al. 2023 (doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2023.03.009) is the closest design: ADNI MCI, Cox, 10-fold CV, c-index 0.74 (0.70-0.79). Published values on ADNI span 0.68 to 0.90, the higher values with richer feature sets.

    Cognitive trajectories (synthetic)

    Mixed model with random intercept and slope: - MMSE declines 1.15 points/year faster in converters (95% CI 1.06-1.24). - ADAS13 worsens 1.77 points/year faster (1.62-1.92).

    ADNI results

    Aggregate results on ADNI (ADNI 1, GO, 2, 3 and 4), published as summary data under the ADNI Data Use Agreement. Models, per-participant predictions and the ADNI table stay local.

    Data. - The ADNIMERGE2 R package, downloaded from LONI IDA on 2026-11-13. ADNIMERGE2 replaces the old single ADNIMERGE.csv; pipelines/prepare_adni.py rebuilds the table from its ADSL, ADRS and ADQS tables. - Hippocampal and intracranial volumes from FreeSurfer, ComBat-harmonised across scanners (ADSP_PHC_T1_FS). - CSF Aβ42, t-tau and p-tau181 from Roche Elecsys (UPENN). - Imaging and CSF are the record closest to enrolment, within 180 days.

    Cohort. - 1,271 participants had MCI at baseline. 125 had no follow-up diagnosis and were excluded, which leaves 1,146 participants and 420 conversions to dementia. - Observed at baseline: hippocampal volume for 1,061, CSF for 716, APOE for 1,135.

    Calibration. Predicted conversion by 36 months follows the observed (Kaplan-Meier) one across fifths of risk: 0.05/0.00, 0.10/0.08, 0.20/0.22, 0.44/0.49, 0.77/0.75, with about 230 participants per fifth.

    Comparison ✓. Wang et al. 2023 (doi:10.1016/j.jclinepi.2023.03.009) report a c-index of 0.74 with clinical variables. Saeed et al. 2024 (doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0314725) report 0.82-0.90 with multimodal random survival forests. Our clinical baseline (0.69) and full models (0.83) sit inside that range.

    The three flexible and linear models reach the same discrimination. A ridge Cox model on baseline values is as good as the forests here, so it stays the model behind the agent.

    Caveat ⚠. CDR-SB and FAQ measure daily function, and functional impairment is part of the clinical definition of dementia. Part of their predictive weight is therefore built into the outcome. The baseline model without them (0.69) is the honest lower bound.

    Do the MR candidates predict conversion in ADNI? Mostly no. Each molecule measured at baseline (within 180 days of enrolment) was tested in its own Cox model of conversion. The molecule was inverse-normal transformed, so hazard ratios are per SD, and each model was adjusted for age, sex and APOE4. FDR was computed across all molecules of each platform.

    Platform MCI participants (conversions) Molecules tested FDR < 0.05 MR candidates measured Candidates at p < 0.05 Enrichment vs other molecules Same direction as MR
    CSF proteins, SomaScan 7k (CruchagaLab) 389 (170) 7,008 aptamers 81 (78 genes) 37 genes, 48 aptamers 4 / 48 (others: 12%) Mann-Whitney p = 0.49 19 / 48
    Serum NMR metabolites (ADMC Nightingale) 786 (342) 250 0 6 0 / 6 (others: 14%) p = 0.98 4 / 6
    • The screen does detect signal. The CSF proteins most associated with conversion are markers of neuronal and synaptic injury: the 14-3-3 family (YWHAG, YWHAZ, YWHAB, YWHAE) and neurofilament heavy chain (NEFH), with HR 1.5-1.8 per SD. They act as a positive control for the pipeline.
    • The candidates do not stand out. Taken together, the 48 aptamers of the MR candidates are no more associated with conversion than the rest of the proteome. Their direction agrees with MR in 19 of 48, which is chance level.
    • ACE is the exception ✓. Higher CSF ACE predicts lower conversion: HR 0.76 per SD, p = 0.0006, which survives Bonferroni across the 48 candidate aptamers. MR points the same way: genetically higher ACE means lower AD risk.
    • Two discordant candidates. CD2AP (HR 0.80) and ACHE (HR 1.28) are nominally associated in the direction opposite to MR.

    Why a negative result is plausible ⚠. Several differences separate the two analyses: - Tissue. MR used plasma protein levels; ADNI measures CSF. - Question. MR asks about lifetime risk of AD; ADNI asks about progression from MCI over a few years, a stage where markers of ongoing injury dominate. - Instruments. Most MR hits rest on a single cis variant at a known GWAS locus, without colocalisation. - Power. 389 participants detect only fairly large effects.

    So the MR candidates are not validated as markers of progression; ACE is the one worth following up. Full tables: results/adni_public/omics_proteins.csv and omics_metabolites.csv.

    Multimodal fusion: what do biomarkers add to the clinical prediction?

    Blocks. - Clinical: age, education, APOE4, MMSE, ADAS13, CDR-SB, FAQ. - MRI: hippocampus and ICV, plus regional FreeSurfer volumes (amygdala, ventricles) and the thickness of seven AD-signature regions. - CSF: Aβ42, t-tau, p-tau. - CSF proteomics: SomaScan 7k, reduced to 20 principal components. - Serum metabolomics: NMR, reduced to 10 components.

    Method. - Imputation, scaling and PCA are fitted inside each training fold. - Participants without a block get zeros for it, plus an indicator. This way all 1,146 participants are kept, although only 389 have proteomics and 785 have metabolomics. - Differences against the clinical model come from a paired bootstrap on the same participants.

    • Clinical scales carry most of the information. All biomarkers together add about 0.02 in C-index, which is real but small. Regional MRI is the single block that comes closest to adding something on its own.
    • The biology is there, but it overlaps with the clinical scales. Without CDR-SB and FAQ, biomarkers raise the baseline from 0.69 to 0.79. This answers the circularity caveat above: the biomarkers predict conversion independently of the functional scales, but they add little once those scales are known.
    • Complete-case check ⚠. Among the 358 participants with every block, all data together reach 0.81 (0.77-0.84), the same as the clinical model (0.81). The small gain of the full cohort does not show up in this subset.

    Cognitive trajectories (mixed model, 1,146 participants). Converters decline faster than stable MCI: - MMSE: 1.27 points/year faster (1.15-1.39); - ADAS13: 2.85 points/year faster (2.57-3.14); - CDR-SB: 1.07 points/year faster (1.00-1.15).

    ADNI (method notes)

    The same code runs locally on ADNIMERGE. ADNI results will be added here as aggregate tables and figures only, with no per-participant data, as the ADNI Data Use Agreement allows. Trained ADNI models and per-participant predictions always stay in the gitignored results/adni/ and models/.

    Agent

    Tool selection by the local model qwen2.5:7b-instruct (Ollama, CPU) on the 12 golden questions: 11/12 (run on 2026-11-12; median 40 s per question on CPU).

    • The miss. "How much faster does MMSE decline in converters?" went to cohort_summary instead of get_result("trajectories"). The model wrote the variable in lower case, the tool refused it, and the agent returned the refusal rather than a number.
    • Correct answers. Risk questions were answered with the tool's probability and bootstrap interval, for example "31.5% (28.1%-36.1%) at 3 years" for a synthetic profile. The request for a single participant's MMSE was refused.

    In CI, a scripted backend checks routing, refusals (small cells, participant identifiers), provenance and abstention on every run.
